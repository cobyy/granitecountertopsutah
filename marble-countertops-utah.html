Why Utah Homeowners Choose Natural Marble

Quarried marble has a visual depth and crystalline translucency that printed porcelain and engineered quartz imitations cannot duplicate. Light penetrates a fraction of an inch into real calcite crystals before reflecting back, giving Italian Carrara, Calacatta, and Statuario slabs a soft, luminous glow in natural Utah sunlight.

Homeowners and bakers also prize natural marble for its naturally cool surface temperature, which stays several degrees below room temperature and makes an ideal station for rolling pastry dough, tempering chocolate, and prepping baked goods. Whether installed on a statement kitchen island in Salt Lake City, a spa-style master vanity in Draper, or a floor-to-ceiling fireplace surround in Park City and Deer Valley, genuine marble ages gracefully and brings architectural permanence to the room.

40+ Years of Utah Marble Experience. Howard Gerstner has fabricated and installed natural marble across Utah since 1984, including commercial installations at the St. Regis Hotel at Deer Valley and the Huntsman Cancer Institute. Every residential project receives that same precision.

The Craftsmanship Difference: Invisible Seams & Mitered Edges

Marble is softer and more directional in its veining than granite or quartz, which means the skill of your stone fabricator determines how the finished room looks. A rushed layout leaves abrupt vein breaks at corners and wide epoxy joints. Howard handles every stage of marble layout and cutting himself.

Grain Matching & Bookmatching

Before a diamond blade touches your marble, Howard maps the template across the slab so the natural veins flow continuously across L-shaped corner seams, up full-height backsplashes, and down 45-degree mitered waterfall island edges. Strategic layout also maximizes slab yield, often fitting a complete counter or island run onto fewer slabs so you don't pay for unnecessary extra stone.

Honed vs. Polished Finishes

  • Honed (Matte) Marble: Our #1 recommendation for Utah kitchen countertops and islands. A honed finish buffs the stone to a smooth, velvety matte surface with low reflection. Because there is no mirror gloss, minor acid etching from lemon juice or wine blends naturally into the matte finish rather than showing as a dull spot.
  • Polished Marble: A high-gloss mirror finish that brings out the deepest contrast in grey, gold, and espresso veining. Polished marble is an exceptional choice for master bathroom vanities, tub decks, shower walls, and living room fireplace surrounds where acidic kitchen spills are not a factor.

Popular Marble Varieties We Fabricate & Install

  • Carrara Marble (Bianco Carrara): Classic Italian white and soft blue-grey background with fine, feathery grey veining. Timeless, versatile, and the most popular honed marble choice for Utah kitchen remodels and bathroom vanities.
  • Calacatta Marble (Calacatta Gold & Calacatta Borghini): A crisp, warm white field crossed by bold, dramatic, thick grey and gold veining—ideal for statement kitchen islands, mitered waterfall edges, and bookmatched full-height backsplashes.
  • Statuario Marble: Bright white Italian marble Quarried near Carrara, prized for high-contrast, sculptural grey movement that turns an island or master shower wall into a focal point.
  • Crema Marfil & Emperador Marble: Warm Spanish cream, ivory, beige, and rich espresso brown tones that pair naturally with custom wood cabinetry, traditional master baths, and stone fireplace hearths.

How to Care for & Seal Marble Countertops in Utah

Marble is a calcium carbonate stone with a Mohs hardness of 3 to 4. We give every homeowner honest, practical guidance on what living with real marble entails so your surfaces stay beautiful for decades.

  • Commercial-Grade Sealing at Installation: We apply a penetrating commercial stone sealer upon installation to slow liquid absorption and guard against oil, coffee, and red wine stains. Re-applying sealer takes 15 to 20 minutes once or twice a year; if water drops stop beading on the surface, it is time to reseal.
  • Protecting Against Acidic Foods: Sealers block liquid stains, but they do not stop chemical etching when acids (lemon juice, lime, vinegar, tomato sauce, or harsh bathroom cleaners) react with calcium carbonate. Always use cutting boards for citrus prep, wipe spills promptly, and choose a honed finish in active kitchens.
  • Preventing Hard Utah Water Buildup: Wasatch Front water has high calcium and magnesium content. Wipe standing water around sink faucets dry with a soft microfiber towel to prevent crusty mineral rings, and clean daily with warm water and mild dish soap or a pH-neutral stone cleaner.
