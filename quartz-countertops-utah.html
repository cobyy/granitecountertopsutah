What Is Engineered Quartz?

Quartz countertops are composed of 90% to 94% ground natural quartz crystal bound together with polymer resins and pigments. The result is a dense, non-porous surface that pairs the look of stone with the color consistency and zero-sealing convenience that busy Utah households value.

Because quartz is manufactured in controlled batches rather than quarried in raw blocks like granite, marble, or quartzite, it delivers predictable tone and veining across multiple slabs.

The Practical Advantages of Quartz in Utah Kitchens

Utah's hard water is tough on surfaces around kitchen faucets and bathroom sinks. Quartz's non-porous composition prevents water and cooking oils from penetrating the surface, and it never needs periodic sealing.

Where Quartz Excels

  • High-traffic family kitchens where zero sealing maintenance is a top priority
  • Bathroom vanities, kids' baths, and laundry countertops with constant moisture
  • Modern kitchen designs requiring a uniform bright white or concrete grey tone
  • Commercial spaces, clinics, and rental properties requiring a non-porous work surface

Large-Format Porcelain Countertops & Outdoor Slabs

Alongside engineered quartz, we fabricate and install large-format porcelain and sintered stone slabs. Fired in kilns at extreme temperatures without polymer resins, porcelain slabs are non-porous like quartz while offering two key advantages:

  • UV-Stable for Utah Outdoor Kitchens: Unlike resin-bound quartz (which can yellow in direct sunlight), porcelain is 100% UV-stable and freeze-thaw resistant—making it ideal for outdoor kitchens and patio bars in Salt Lake, Utah County, and Park City.
  • High Heat Resistance & Sleek Mitered Profiles: Porcelain handles high temperatures, and Howard fabricates precision 45-degree mitered edges to build thick, bookmatched waterfall islands or sleek full-height shower and fireplace walls.

How to Clean & Care for Quartz Countertops

  • Daily Cleaning: Wipe with warm water, mild dish soap, and a soft microfiber cloth.
  • Always Use Trivets for Hot Pans: Quartz resins can discolor or crack if exposed to direct high heat from a cast-iron skillet, roasting pan, or slow cooker. Always place hot cookware on a trivet or hot pad.
  • Avoid Harsh Chemicals: Skip bleach, oven cleaners, and abrasive scouring pads, which can dull the factory polish.
