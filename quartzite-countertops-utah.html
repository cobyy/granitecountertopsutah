What Is Quartzite? (Natural Quartzite vs. Engineered Quartz)

This is the most common source of confusion in the countertop industry, and it matters when you are choosing a surface for your home. Quartzite is a 100% natural stone—a metamorphic rock formed when quartz-rich sandstone is subjected to extreme heat and pressure deep within the earth, causing the sand grains to recrystallize into a dense, interlocking mineral matrix. Quartz (engineered stone) is a manufactured product made from ground minerals bound with polymer resins.

In practical terms: quartzite is quarried and cut just like granite and marble. Every slab is unique. It has no binders, no resins, and no manufactured elements. Ranked 7 on the Mohs hardness scale—harder than a steel knife blade—it resists scratching and direct kitchen heat exceptionally well.

The "Marble Look, Granite Durability" Stone. Many homeowners love the sweeping, translucent veining of natural marble and want extra scratch resistance for a high-traffic family kitchen. Quartzite varieties like Taj Mahal, Super White, White Macaubas, and Sea Pearl deliver that organic veining in one of the hardest countertop stones on earth.

Why Quartzite Is So Popular Across Utah

Across Utah's luxury home market—particularly in Draper, Sandy, South Jordan, Alpine, Highland, and Park City and Deer Valley—quartzite has become a signature material for custom kitchen islands, waterfall edges, and full-height slab backsplashes. Designers and architects specify it for its translucent crystalline depth, natural movement, and longevity.

Because quartzite is harder than granite, it demands specialized diamond tooling, slower feed rates, and an experienced fabricator to prevent micro-chipping along mitered edges and sink cutouts. Howard Gerstner has been cutting stone in Utah since 1984. He accompanies you in person to Salt Lake wholesale slab yards to inspect full quartzite slabs and handles every cut in-house.

How to Care for & Clean Quartzite Countertops

Quartzite is exceptionally hard, and like all natural stones, keeping its seal intact protects its light crystalline background from daily cooking spills.

Daily Cleaning Routine

  • Wipe Spills Promptly: Clean up cooking oils, wine, and coffee so they do not sit overnight near seams or cutouts.
  • Use Mild Soap & Water: Warm water and a few drops of dish soap or a pH-neutral stone cleaner are ideal for daily care.
  • Dry Thoroughly: Buff around faucets with a dry microfiber cloth to prevent hard Utah water mineral deposits.

Sealing Your Quartzite

We apply a commercial-grade penetrating sealer at installation. Most quartzite countertops benefit from resealing once every 1 to 2 years. You can check your seal anytime with the water test: splash a few drops of water on the counter—if it beads up, the seal is intact; if it darkens the stone within a few minutes, wipe on a fresh coat of sealer.
