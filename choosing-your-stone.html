Buyer's Guide

Choosing Your Countertop Stone

Granite vs. Marble vs. Quartzite vs. Quartz? We break down the pros, cons, and performance of each stone to help you choose the right surface for your Utah home.

At Granite Countertops of Utah, Howard Gerstner has fabricated and installed thousands of custom stone surfaces since 1984. Every stone we work with is premium slab-grade, and the right choice comes down to your space, your cooking habits, and the look you want to wake up to every day.

Quartz vs. Natural Stone (Granite, Marble & Quartzite)

This is the most common question in kitchen remodeling. Do you go with the zero-sealing convenience of engineered quartz, or the one-of-a-kind geological depth of natural granite, marble, or quartzite?

Quartz (Engineered) →

  • Non-porous: Never needs sealing
  • High stain and acid resistance
  • Consistent pattern & color across slabs
  • Tough against everyday chips and scratches

Natural Stone (Granite / Quartzite) →

  • Superior heat resistance for hot pans
  • 100% unique, one-of-a-kind natural slabs
  • Strong resale appeal in Utah luxury homes
  • Luminous crystalline depth that cannot be printed

Natural Marble vs. Natural Quartzite

Both natural marble and natural quartzite deliver sweeping, organic veining that engineered stone cannot replicate—but they behave very differently in a kitchen:

Natural Marble (Carrara / Calacatta) →

  • Unmatched classic Italian white & grey beauty
  • Naturally cool surface ideal for baking & pastry
  • Honed (matte) finish conceals everyday etching
  • Showstopping for islands, vanities & fireplaces

Natural Quartzite (Taj Mahal / Sea Pearl) →

  • Harder than granite (Mohs 7–8)
  • True marble-like movement without acid etching
  • Handles heavy cast-iron cookware and high heat
  • Ideal for high-traffic family kitchen islands

Quartz vs. Porcelain

Large-format sintered porcelain slabs are a strong option alongside engineered quartz, particularly for outdoor kitchens, fireplace walls, and modern thin-profile mitered islands. Explore our Quartz & Porcelain guide →

Choose Quartz if...

  • You want a full-body 3cm edge profile ( eased, ogee, or bullnose).
  • You prefer warm white backgrounds and subtle veining.
  • You want the most proven, low-maintenance indoor kitchen surface.

Choose Porcelain if...

  • You are building an outdoor kitchen (Porcelain is 100% UV-stable).
  • You want a sleek modern aesthetic or mitered waterfall island.
  • You want maximum heat resistance in a man-made slab.

Exotic & Specialized Stones

Soapstone: The Velvet Stone

Soapstone is a non-porous natural stone prized for its heat resistance and smooth, tactile feel. It develops a natural patina over time, and many homeowners treat it with food-safe mineral oil for a rich, dark charcoal finish.

Onyx: The Translucent Showpiece

Onyx is a semi-precious natural stone known for its translucency. When backlit with LED panels, an onyx bar top, fireplace feature, or powder room vanity glows with dramatic depth.

Limestone: Soft & Architectural

Limestone offers a warm, matte, earthy palette suited for master bath vanities, tub surrounds, and fireplace hearths where organic texture is the priority.
